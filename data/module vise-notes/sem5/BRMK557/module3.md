## Module 3: Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) & Patenting Process

### Intellectual Property: Concept and Role in Economic Development

- **Intellectual Property (IP)** refers to creations of the mind — inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, symbols, names, and images used in commerce. The legal rights granted over these creations are **Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)**.
- **Rationale for IPR**: IP is an intangible asset; without protection, others can copy it freely (free-rider problem). IPR gives the creator a temporary **exclusive right (monopoly)** that acts as a reward for innovation and an incentive for further creativity.
- **Role of IP in economic development**:
  - Encourages investment in R&D by guaranteeing returns (innovation incentive).
  - Transforms knowledge into tradable assets — licensing, franchising, technology transfer.
  - Attracts foreign direct investment (FDI) and technology collaborations.
  - Promotes commercialization of university research (patents → licensing → start-ups).
  - Builds national competitiveness — countries with strong IP regimes attract more innovation.
  - Creates jobs and fosters start-up ecosystems (patent-protected products).
  - Protects traditional knowledge and indigenous products (GI, traditional knowledge databases).
- **Economic principle**: IPR balances two competing interests — the creator's right to reward (monopoly) versus society's right to access knowledge (diffusion). The balance is achieved through limited duration of protection and disclosure requirements.
- **WIPO (World Intellectual Property Organization)**: UN agency based in Geneva, administers international IP treaties (Paris Convention, Berne Convention, PCT, Madrid Protocol). India is a member.

### Types of Intellectual Property

| Type of IP | Protects | Governing law in India | Duration | Registration needed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Patent | Inventions (products/processes) | Patents Act, 1970 (amended 2005) | 20 years from filing | Yes |
| Copyright | Literary, artistic, musical, dramatic works, software, sound recordings | Copyright Act, 1957 | Author's life + 60 years | Not mandatory (automatic) |
| Trademark | Brand names, logos, symbols distinguishing goods/services | Trade Marks Act, 1999 | 10 years, renewable | Yes |
| Industrial Design | Visual/ornamental features of an article | Designs Act, 2000 | 10 years + 5 years extension | Yes |
| Geographical Indication (GI) | Indication identifying goods from a specific region | Geographical Indications of Goods Act, 1999 | 10 years, renewable | Yes |
| Trade Secret | Confidential business information, formulas | Common law/contract law | As long as kept secret | No |

- **Trade secrets**: e.g., Coca-Cola formula, Google's search algorithm; protected by confidentiality agreements (NDA); no registration, lost once disclosed.
- **Know the difference**: patents protect *inventions* (technical solutions); copyrights protect *expression of ideas* (not the idea itself); trademarks protect *brand identity*; designs protect *appearance*; GIs protect *origin-linked reputation*.

### Indian IP Governance Framework

- **CGPDTM — Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks** (under DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry, Government of India). It is the apex body administering IP in India and is headquartered in Mumbai.
- **Functions of CGPDTM**: administers the Patents Act, Designs Act, Trade Marks Act, and GI Act; conducts examinations, grants registrations, maintains registers, and oversees the **Patent Information System (PIS)** at Nagpur.
- **Subordinate bodies under CGPDTM**:
  - **Patent offices** (Kolkata — head office, Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai) with jurisdiction over states.
  - **Trade Marks Registry** (Mumbai head office + branch offices).
  - **GI Registry** (Chennai).
  - **Design offices** co-located with patent offices.
- **Copyright governance**: Copyright Office under the **Copyright Office / Registrar of Copyrights** (Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade - DPIIT), separate from CGPDTM.
- **Other stakeholders**: IPAB (Intellectual Property Appellate Board — abolished in 2021; appeals now go to High Courts), CSIR (major patent filer in India), NRDC (National Research Development Corporation — commercializes government inventions), and **National IPR Policy 2016** — India's comprehensive IP strategy ("Creative India: Innovative India").

### Patents: Definition and Conditions for Patentability

- **Patent**: An exclusive statutory right granted by the government to an inventor for a limited term (20 years in India) in exchange for full public disclosure of the invention. It is a **negative right** — it allows the owner to *exclude others* from making, using, selling, or importing the invention without permission; it is not a right to practice the invention.
- **What is patentable**: a new *product* or *process* involving an inventive step and capable of industrial application (Sec 2(1)(j), Patents Act 1970).
- **Three conditions for patentability** (PYQ 2023 Q5a — memorize):
  1. **Novelty**: The invention is new — not published, used, or known anywhere in the world (publicly disclosed) before the filing date. The disclosure must not have happened through any publication, sale, or prior use anywhere.
  2. **Non-obviousness / Inventive Step**: The invention is not obvious to a person skilled in the art — i.e., a meaningful technical advance over existing knowledge; a person with ordinary skill in the field would not arrive at it easily. Indian definition: "a feature that makes the invention not obvious to a person skilled in the art."
  3. **Industrial Applicability (Utility)**: The invention must be capable of being made or used in an industry (practical utility). Pure scientific theories, mathematical methods, and aesthetic creations lack industrial applicability.
- **Supporting requirements**: enablement (complete specification must describe the invention fully enough for a skilled person to reproduce it — Sec 10), and "best method" disclosure.
- **Grace period**: India (like most countries) follows **absolute novelty** — even the inventor's own publication before filing destroys novelty. So publish after filing, never before (unlike US's 1-year grace period).

### Non-Patentable Subject Matter: Sections 3 and 4 (Indian Patents Act)

- **Section 3 — inventions not patentable** (PYQ 2023 Q5a — the most repeated list):
  - **3(a)**: Frivolous inventions or those contrary to natural laws.
  - **3(b)**: Inventions contrary to public order, morality, or causing serious prejudice to human, animal, plant life/health or environment.
  - **3(c)**: Mere discovery of a scientific principle or formulation of an abstract theory (knowledge, not invention).
  - **3(d)**: Mere discovery of a new form of a known substance that does not enhance its known efficacy; also new properties/uses of known substances, mere new uses of known processes/machines. (The famous **Novartis/Glivec case** — beta crystalline form of imatinib mesylate held not patentable under 3(d).)
  - **3(e)**: Mere admixture/combination of known substances (mere aggregation producing no new synergistic effect) and mere arrangement/rearrangement/duplication of known devices.
  - **3(f)**: Mere arrangement or rearrangement or duplication of known devices each functioning independently.
  - **3(g)**: Method of agriculture or horticulture.
  - **3(h)**: Method of treatment of humans/animals by surgery, therapy, or diagnosis (medical methods).
  - **3(i)**: Plants and animals in whole or any part (including seeds, varieties, species) and essentially biological processes for their production. (Note: microorganisms, like bacteria, ARE patentable under the Act; plant varieties are protected separately under PPV&FR Act 2001.)
  - **3(j)**: Mathematical or business methods, computer programs *per se*, and algorithms. (Software patents: only software with technical application — a "computer program with technical effect" embedded in hardware may be allowed; pure code is not.)
  - **3(k)**: Literary, dramatic, musical, or artistic works, cinematographic films, television productions (these go to copyright).
  - **3(l)**: Mere scheme, rule, or method of performing a mental act.
  - **3(m)**: Presentation of information.
  - **3(n)**: Topography of integrated circuits (protected separately under Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design Act 2000).
  - **3(o)**: Traditional knowledge or aggregation/duplication of properties of traditionally known components (added 2002 — protects Indian traditional knowledge).
- **Section 4**: Inventions relating to **atomic energy** (as notified under the Atomic Energy Act 1962) are not patentable.
- **Exam tip**: Students often get asked "any 5-8 items from Section 3" — the most asked are 3(d), 3(e), 3(i), 3(j), 3(k), 3(b), 3(h), 3(o).

### Patent Drafting: Provisional vs Complete Specification

- **Specification**: The written document that fully describes the invention — its title, field, background, prior art, problem, summary, detailed description, drawings, and claims. It is the heart of the patent.
- **Two stages of filing in India**:
  - **Provisional specification**: A preliminary filing describing the invention broadly (the idea/principle) without full details or claims. Purpose: establish an **earlier priority date**, secure the filing date while the inventor develops the invention, and allow "patent pending" status.
  - **Complete specification**: Full description — title, abstract, field of invention, background (prior art), objects, summary, detailed description with drawings, and **claims** (the legal boundary of protection). Must be filed within **12 months** of the provisional filing, else the application is abandoned.
- Comparison table (PYQ 2023 Q5b — direct question):

| Feature | Provisional Specification | Complete Specification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Content | Broad description of idea | Full detailed disclosure + claims + abstract |
| Claims | Not required | Required (define scope of protection) |
| Purpose | Early priority date | Examination and grant |
| Deadline | Filing date | Must follow within 12 months |
| Status | "Patent pending" only | Basis of grant |
| Priority | Secures date | Scope decided by complete spec |

- **Claims**: The numbered sentences that define the legal scope of the patent. **Independent claims** (stand alone, broad) and **dependent claims** (refer back, narrower). Claims are the most important part — everything not claimed is not protected.
- **Parts of complete specification (Sec 10)**: title, preamble, field of invention, background/prior art and problem, summary of invention, brief description of drawings, detailed description, claims, abstract (~150 words).
- **Important**: The complete specification must disclose the **best method** of performing the invention known to the inventor, and must enable a person skilled in the art to perform it without undue experimentation (enablement requirement).

### Prior Art Search

- **Prior art**: All publicly available knowledge (patents, publications, products, sales, public use, conference papers) that existed before the filing date and is relevant to the invention.
- **Purpose of prior art search** (PYQ 2024 Q5a): to determine novelty and inventive step, avoid wasting effort/money on a non-patentable invention, avoid infringing existing patents, and to draft better claims (narrower claims around known art).
- **Steps in prior art search**:
  1. Break the invention into concepts/keywords (plus synonyms, synonyms across languages).
  2. Classify the technical field (IPC/CPC classification codes) to find related patents.
  3. Search patent databases — **Indian patents (ipindia.gov.in — InPASS)**, **Google Patents**, **WIPO PATENTSCOPE** (PCT database), **Espacenet** (EPO), **USPTO Patent Full-Text**.
  4. Search non-patent literature — journals (IEEE, Scopus, Google Scholar), conference proceedings, theses.
  5. Combine results, remove duplicates, screen by relevance.
  6. Analyze the closest hits for novelty conflict and for claim overlap.
  7. Document the search (query, date, databases, hits) — this itself becomes evidence of due diligence.
- **IPC (International Patent Classification)**: hierarchical code (A-H sections; e.g., Section H = Electricity) used to index patents; helps locate similar inventions. CPC (Cooperative Patent Classification) is the US-EPO joint scheme.
- **Search tips**: use claim text, not just abstract; check family members (same invention filed in multiple countries); search backward citations of found patents; include non-English art.
- **Outcome of search**: either novelty confirmed (proceed to file) or anticipation found (re-design, narrow claims, or abandon).

### Patent Filing Process in India (flow)

```
[DIAGRAM: Indian patent lifecycle
Conceive invention --> Prior art search --> Draft provisional spec (file)
  --> within 12 months: file complete specification
  --> File request for examination (RFE) --> Examiner searches & examines
  --> Examination report / objections --> Reply & amend claims
  --> Acceptance --> Grant + publication in Patent Journal
  --> 20-year term (annual renewal fees) --> possible opposition/infringement suits
Pre-grant opposition and post-grant opposition (Sec 25) possible at two stages
]
```

- **Key stages**: Filing application (Form 1), publication (automatic after 18 months), **request for examination (RFE — within 48 months of filing)**; examination by the Controller; compliance; **grant**; renewal fees each year (from 3rd year).
- **Opposition**: pre-grant opposition (by any person, before grant) and post-grant opposition (within 12 months of grant) — Sec 25.
- **PCT route**: For international protection, file a **PCT (Patent Cooperation Treaty)** application (WIPO) — a single filing valid internationally; national phase entry within 30/31 months. Indian inventors also get a **reduced fee (80% discount) if filing as a sole/natural-person inventor**.

### Patent Infringement

- **Patent infringement**: Unauthorized making, using, selling, offering for sale, or importing a patented invention within the country during the patent term, without the patent owner's license.
- **Types of infringement** (PYQ 2024 Q5b):
  - **Direct infringement**: literal violation — the accused product/process literally falls within the scope of at least one claim.
  - **Indirect infringement**: includes:
    - **Inducement to infringe**: actively encouraging another party to infringe (e.g., selling instructions).
    - **Contributory infringement**: supplying a component/part that has no substantial non-infringing use and is specifically intended for the patented invention.
  - **Doctrine of Equivalents**: infringement even when there is no literal match, if the accused product performs substantially the same function, in substantially the same way, to achieve substantially the same result (FWR — function, way, result test). Example: replacing a metal shaft with a nylon one of equal strength.
- **Read the claims first** — infringement analysis always begins with claim construction (interpreting claim language).
- **Defenses to infringement**: non-infringement (no claim overlap), patent invalidity (attacking novelty/inventive step), experimental/research use exemption (bolar-like provisions), exhaustion of rights (licensed goods), prior user rights.
- **Remedies for infringement in India (Sec 108-111)**: injunction (interim/final), damages or account of profits, and seizure/destruction of infringing goods. Suits are filed in District Court or High Court.
- **Example**: the long-running **Bajaj vs TVS** and **Dabur vs Emami** disputes are standard Indian examples of patent/suit litigation; international: Apple vs Samsung smartphone patent wars.

### Quick Revision Points (Module 3)

- IPR = exclusive rights over creations of mind; balance of reward vs diffusion.
- Know the 6 IP types + terms (patent 20 yrs, copyright life+60, TM 10 renewable, design 10+5, GI 10 renewable).
- Patentability: Novelty + Inventive Step + Industrial Applicability.
- Section 3 highlights: 3(d) (new form of known substance/Novartis), 3(e) (mere admixture), 3(h) (medical methods), 3(i) (plants/animals), 3(j) (algorithms/computer programs per se), 3(k) (artistic works); Section 4 = atomic energy.
- Provisional vs complete spec: 12-month deadline, claims only in complete.
- Prior art search: ipindia/InPASS, Google Patents, PATENTSCOPE, Espacenet; document the search.
- Infringement: direct, induced, contributory, Doctrine of Equivalents (FWR).
