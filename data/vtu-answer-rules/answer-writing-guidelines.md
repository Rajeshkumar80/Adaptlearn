# VTU Semester End Examination (SEE) — Answer Writing & Scoring Guidelines

This document synthesizes official Visvesvaraya Technological University (VTU) evaluation standards, faculty valuation conventions, and scheme-of-evaluation practices across VTU-affiliated autonomous and non-autonomous engineering colleges.

---

## 1. Standard Theory Question Answer Structure

For theory-based questions (typically 6 to 10 marks), VTU valuation schemes award marks based on structural clarity. Follow the **4-Tier Structure**:

1. **Definition / Concise Statement (1–2 Marks)**:
   - Provide a formal 2–3 line technical definition highlighting key terms.
   - Use standard terminology directly from recommended syllabus textbooks.
2. **Core Technical Explanation (3–4 Marks)**:
   - Break explanations into bullet points or numbered steps with bold headers.
   - Avoid long monolithic paragraphs; valuation key sheets allocate 1 mark per major concept point.
3. **Labeled Schematic / Architecture Diagram (2–3 Marks)**:
   - Always include a neat, hand-drawn style diagram even if not explicitly demanded by the prompt.
   - Use block diagrams, flowcharts, or state transition diagrams with complete axis/node labeling.
4. **Concrete Example / Industrial Use Case (1–2 Marks)**:
   - Provide a real-world scenario, code snippet, or algorithm application showing practical relevance.

---

## 2. Diagram Marking & Presentation Conventions

- **Visual Separation**: Center diagrams and enclose them in clean boxes.
- **Labeling Rules**:
  - Labels must be placed horizontally outside the diagram boundaries with clean pointer arrows.
  - Unlabeled diagrams receive up to **50% mark penalty** during valuation key matching.
- **Legends & Notations**: Define mathematical symbols, variables, or signal names directly below the diagram.

---

## 3. Numerical & Mathematical Derivation Format

For mathematical derivations, algorithmic step-throughs, and quantitative problems:

1. **Given Data & To Find**: Clearly list input parameters with proper SI units or system units at the top.
2. **Formula State**: Explicitly write the generic formula before substituting values.
3. **Step-by-Step Substitution**: Show intermediate algebraic/arithmetic calculations; VTU evaluators award step marks for valid intermediate steps even if final arithmetic contains errors.
4. **Final Result Presentation**: Highlight final numerical answers inside a box, clearly stating units (e.g., `\(\text{Throughput} = 4.5 \text{ Mbps}\)`).

---

## 4. Marks & Course Outcome (CO) Mapping to Question Parts

VTU 2022 Scheme question papers are divided into 5 Modules with internal choice (Module 1: Q1 or Q2, Module 2: Q3 or Q4, etc.):

- **Question Splitting Pattern**:
  - **Option A**: 10 marks (Single long derivation/design question - CO mapped).
  - **Option B**: 6 marks + 4 marks split (e.g., Q1a: Theory + Diagram [6M], Q1b: Numerical/Example [4M]).
  - **Option C**: 5 marks + 5 marks split or 7 marks + 3 marks split.
- **Bloom's Taxonomy Levels in Question Papers**:
  - **L1 (Remembering)**: List, Define, State (typically 2–3 marks).
  - **L2 (Understanding)**: Explain, Differentiate, Describe (typically 4–6 marks).
  - **L3 (Applying) / L4 (Analyzing)**: Design, Solve, Derive, Compare (typically 6–10 marks).

---

## 5. Distinction / High-Score Checklist for Examiners

- **Comparison Tables**: Always present "Differentiate between X and Y" in a 3-column table format (`Parameter | System X | System Y`). Avoid paragraph comparisons.
- **Algorithm Writing**: Present algorithms with clear Input, Output, and Pseudocode steps.
- **Clean Handwriting & Margin Integrity**: Keep question numbers prominently displayed in left margins (`Q3(a)`).
