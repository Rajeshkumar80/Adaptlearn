## 🎯 Course Objectives

1. Understand biological structures, cell functions, and essential biomolecules from an engineering perspective.
2. Explore industrial and technological applications of biomolecules in materials, health, and energy.
3. Learn how anatomical principles inspire bioengineering and robotic designs.
4. Analyze nature-inspired mechanisms, bio-mimicry, and surface phenomena.
5. Understand modern trends in bioengineering including bio-printing, biocomputing, and tissue scaffolds.

## 📚 Module-Wise Syllabus

### Module 1: Cell — The Basic Unit of Life
- Introduction to Cell Structure and Function: Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic cells
- Cellular organelles and their functional analogs to engineering systems
- Stem Cells: Types (Embryonic, Adult, Induced Pluripotent), Potency, and Medical Applications
- Biomolecules: Classification, Chemical structure, and Biological functions of Carbohydrates, Lipids, Proteins, and Nucleic Acids
- Specialized Biomolecules: Enzymes (Biocatalysts), Vitamins, and Hormones in biochemical regulation

### Module 2: Application of Biomolecules in Engineering
- Carbohydrates: Cellulose-based water filtration membranes, Bioplastics (PLA, PHA) synthesis and biodegradation
- Proteins: Recombinant protein production, Silk fibroin in structural materials, Food science applications
- Lipids: Biodiesel synthesis via transesterification, Surfactants, and Detergents
- Nucleic Acids: DNA/RNA in molecular diagnostics, PCR amplification, mRNA vaccines
- Enzymes in Industry: Biosensors (glucose oxidase), Food processing, Textile processing, Bio-detergents

### Module 3: Adaptation of Anatomical Principles for Bioengineering Design
- Human Anatomy as an Engineering Blueprint: Organ system analogies
- Brain as a Central Processing Unit (CPU): Neural network architectures and neuromorphic computing
- Eye as an Optical Camera: Lens mechanics, Photoreceptors (rods/cones), and Digital image sensors
- Heart as a Mechanical Pump: Cardiac cycle, Hemodynamics, and Artificial hearts/valves
- Lungs as Gas Exchangers: Diffusion principles, Ventilators, and Oxygenator designs
- Kidneys as Filtration Systems: Dialysis membranes and Hemodialysis machine engineering

### Module 4: Nature-Bioinspired Materials and Mechanisms
- Bio-mimicry: Learning from nature's designs to solve complex engineering challenges
- Echolocation in bats/dolphins and application in RADAR / SONAR systems
- Photosynthesis: Light-harvesting complexes and Dye-sensitized solar cells / Photovoltaics
- Aerodynamic inspiration: Bird flight aerodynamics and Aircraft winglet design
- Surface Phenomena: Lotus Leaf effect (Superhydrophobicity and self-cleaning surfaces)
- Plant Burrs and invention of Velcro fastening mechanism
- Sharkskin denticles for hydrodynamic friction-reducing swimsuits and ship hulls
- Kingfisher bird beak morphology inspiring aerodynamic Shinkansen bullet train nose design
- Blood Substitutes: Hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers (HBOCs) and Perfluorocarbons

### Module 5: Trends in Bioengineering & Future Technologies
- Musculoskeletal Systems as Engineering Scaffolds: Biomechanics, Bone density, Artificial limbs / Prosthetics
- Tissue Engineering: Tri-component tissue engineering (cells, scaffolds, signaling factors)
- 3D Bioprinting: Extrusion and inkjet bioprinting of skin, cartilage, blood vessels, and cultured meat
- Electronic Nose (e-Nose) and Electronic Tongue (e-Tongue) in food quality assurance and hazard detection
- DNA Origami: Nanoscale self-assembly and drug delivery nanobots
- Biocomputing: DNA computing algorithms, Logic gates using biological molecules, Synthetic biology overview
